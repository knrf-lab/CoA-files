The CPU performance equation

CPU cycles = IC × CPI
CPU time = IC × CPI × T = (IC × CPI) ÷ f
CPI = (CPU time × f) ÷ IC
Performance ∝ 1 ÷ execution time
Speedup = old time ÷ new time (> 1 means the new one is faster)
IC = instruction count, CPI = average cycles per instruction, f = clock rate, T = 1/f

A faster clock alone does not guarantee a faster program: instruction count, CPI, memory behaviour and the program itself all matter.

Example cheat sheet P13

4 × 10⁸ instructions. Old: CPI 2.5 at 2 GHz. New: CPI 3 at 2.5 GHz.
Old = 4×10⁸ × 2.5 ÷ 2×10⁹ = 0.5 s
New = 4×10⁸ × 3 ÷ 2.5×10⁹ = 0.48 s
Speedup = 0.5 ÷ 0.48 = 1.042: the new CPU wins despite its worse CPI.

Example: a faster clock that doesn't help

2 × 10⁹ instructions. A: CPI 1.5 at 3 GHz → 3×10⁹ ÷ 3×10⁹ = 1.0 s. B: CPI 1.2 at 2.4 GHz → 2.4×10⁹ ÷ 2.4×10⁹ = 1.0 s. Same time: B's better CPI cancels A's faster clock.

Instruction pipelining

Pipelining overlaps the stages of the fetch–decode–execute cycle, like an assembly line: while instruction 1 is in stage 2, instruction 2 is in stage 1. It increases throughput (instructions finished per second); it does not make one instruction finish faster.

Cycle123456789
Instr 1S1S2S3S4S5S6
Instr 2S1S2S3S4S5S6
Instr 3S1S2S3S4S5S6
Instr 4S1S2S3S4S5S6

6 stages, 4 instructions: 6 + 4 − 1 = 9 cycles (slide S4-5 #14). Without pipelining: 6 × 4 = 24 cycles.

k = stages, n = instructions (tasks), τ = pipeline clock cycle, tn = time for one instruction without pipelining (= k·τ if the stages are equal)
Pipelined time = (k + n − 1) × τ
Non-pipelined time = n × tn
Speedup S = n·tn ÷ ((k + n − 1)·τ)
Equal stages: S = n·k ÷ (k + n − 1) → approaches k as n grows.
With s stall cycles: (k + n − 1 + s) × τ
✔ Why k + n − 1? The first instruction needs k cycles to go through every stage ("filling the pipe"). After that, one instruction finishes every cycle: n − 1 more.

Worked example quiz Q7, prof's solution

Given: k = 5, τ = 40 ns, n = 800, tn = k × τ = 200 ns.
Step 1 Non-pipelined = n × tn = 800 × 200 = 160,000 ns
Step 2 Pipelined = (k + n − 1) × τ = (5 + 800 − 1) × 40 = 804 × 40 = 32,160 ns
Step 3 S = 160,000 ÷ 32,160 ≈ 4.975 (close to the maximum 5)

Example S4-5 exercise 5

Non-pipelined task 200 ns; 5 segments of 40 ns; 200 tasks.
200 × 200 = 40,000 ns vs (5 + 199) × 40 = 8,160 ns → S = 4.90

Example with stalls cheat sheet P7

k = 4, τ = 25 ns, n = 100, tn = 100 ns. S = 10,000 ÷ (103 × 25) = 3.883. With 6 stalls: 10,000 ÷ (109 × 25) = 3.670.
